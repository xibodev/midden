# Produce useful, supportable prose

Deliver editable prose: investigate, write, check, revise, and save it.
Describing the work is not delivery.
For opportunity-finding without a writing request, use investigation instead.

Read the shared [source guide](../midden-shared/sources.md) and
[dependency guide](../midden-shared/tools.md).

## Work from intent and existing material

Establish the reader, intended takeaway, medium, and destination from the
request and files. Inspect the named folder's drafts, notes, and collections;
resume the actual draft, not merely a catalog entry. Preserve useful human
edits. Revise the agreed file rather than scattering near-identical outputs.
Keep a brief or outline only when it helps.

For a troubleshooting tutorial, teach the useful lesson from failure rather
than inventing a successful result.

## Support the piece you can honestly write

Execute focused core reads and searches where session evidence is relevant.
Read the later outcome and corrections before constructing a narrative arc.
Reuse collected support and counterevidence; add missing material without
deleting an existing `sources` collection. Keep interpretation separate.
Preserve planned, attempted, reported, and verified distinctions in the prose.
A statement that checks passed remains reported, even after you inspect it.

Choose structure for the reader:

| Form | Useful shape |
|---|---|
| Article | A clear claim, supporting evidence, explanation, limitations, and takeaway |
| Tutorial | Prerequisites, a concrete goal, runnable steps, expected observations, failure handling, and verification |
| Case study | Context, choices and tradeoffs, actions actually taken, observed outcome, limits, and transferable lesson |

Run tutorial examples only after checking them for safety and authorization,
in a scoped working area. Inspect their real output. Label commands or claims
that were not reproduced, including platform and dependency limitations.
Supplemental research needs its own sources; unsupported details are revised,
qualified, or removed.

Use the source guide's exact-locator note for substantive claims. Link readable
citations to it; retain full opaque IDs/digests or explicit collection pointers,
not decorative abbreviations. Check direct
quotations against the actual source wording and context. Paraphrases are not
verbatim quotations. Quotation marks around a concept, coined label, or ironic
term do not by themselves claim somebody said it; audit meaning and attribution,
not every pair of quote characters.

## Deliver the revised artifact

Write the article or tutorial as Markdown unless the operator requests another
format. When a rendered artifact is wanted, run the available renderer, open
the result, and revise the same source and output target. Check the introduction
against the actual ending, citations against the collected records, and examples
against observed behavior.

Reopen the saved file before handing it over. Report its path, audience and
scope, and any material unverified claim or missing requested format. A tight
budget calls for a smaller sourced, saved piece or an explicitly incomplete
draft, not an unsupported chat article called finished. Local files remain
unpublished unless the operator separately authorizes sharing.
